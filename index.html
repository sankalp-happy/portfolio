RDSO Documents

A weekly ingest pass for Southern Railway, built around the one signal that moves when a standard is revised.

RDSO engineering standards live on RailNet. Getting them to engineers over the internet needs an ingest pass that can tell a revised document from one it has already published. The source portal reuses a document's URL when the document is revised. Same link, new bytes.

So the obvious signal is the wrong one. The first build watched the link set, found it unchanged every week, and propagated nothing while standards were being revised underneath it. Nothing errored. That failure sent us looking for a channel that moves.

The ETag moves. The crawler stores one per URL, and an ETag delta is the only honest evidence a document changed. Only the changed rows cross back out of RailNet, never the whole database. Nothing is ever deleted. A superseded standard goes to Archive.

SwitchBoard

An LLM gateway that keeps three providers behind one endpoint, and recognises a question it has already answered.

One endpoint in front of Groq, Google and Anthropic. A semantic cache answers repeat questions instead of the provider, and keys rotate the moment one hits a rate limit. The application layer never learns that a provider degraded.

The cache compares prompts by cosine similarity rather than by hash, which is the only reason it catches a question asked in different words. Swapping a provider is a config change, not a refactor.

PharmaGuard

Prescribing guidance read off a genomic file, in a system where the model does not get to make the call.

A genomic VCF goes in and CPIC-guided prescribing guidance comes out. A deterministic risk engine makes every clinical call. The model writes one thing, the explanation a clinician reads, and it writes that after the decision is already final.

That constraint is the architecture, not a precaution bolted on afterwards. It is also the only property that makes an LLM admissible here. If the model fails, the clinician loses the prose and keeps the recommendation.

Limits What is not validated, and what I have not done.

Most of what is on this site is hackathon- or coursework-scale. SwitchBoard is the exception. It has served real traffic, and I will stand behind the numbers in Fig. 2.

PharmaGuard has not been through clinical validation. Do not read it as if it had. It shows that the boundary in Fig. 3 can be built. It is not evidence that the recommendations behind it are safe to act on.

The RDSO crawler ran weekly on one small server inside one network. It is correct, not fast, and it has never been asked to be. I have also written it up here from memory of a system I no longer have access to.

I have not trained anything at scale and I have not published. The two reproductions under Projects are the closest I have come to research, and they are small. My work starts at the model boundary and goes outward. Routing, retrieval, ingest, failover, evaluation. I would rather write that down than let a résumé imply otherwise.
