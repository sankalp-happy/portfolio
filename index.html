I work at the boundary where a model stops being allowed to decide.

Gateways, serving, retrieval, ingest, failover. This half of an LLM product decides whether the model is safe to put in front of anyone.

I build the parts around the model. Provider routing with failover, and a cache that recognises a question it has already answered in different words. Retrieval that finds product codes as well as meaning. Ingest that can tell a revised document from one it has already published. Serving tuned to hold more requests on the same GPUs. Then the FastAPI and React that puts any of it in front of a person.

Right now I tune LLM serving at Kapture CX. Before that I built the ingest crawler for a Southern Railway document system, where the source portal reused a URL every time it revised a file. On my own I built an LLM gateway that has served real traffic, and a prescribing system where a deterministic engine makes every clinical call and the model only writes the explanation.

Most of what breaks in an LLM product is not the model. It is the retry nobody wrote, the cache that compares by hash, the crawler watching a signal that never moves.

Southern Railway · Remote · March to June 2026

RDSO engineering standards live on RailNet and had to reach engineers over the internet. The source portal reuses a document's URL when it revises the file. So the first build watched the link set, found it unchanged every week, and propagated nothing while standards were being revised underneath it.

The ETag is the one thing that moves. The crawler stores one per URL and treats a delta as the evidence a document changed. Only changed rows leave RailNet, on a weekly cron, and nothing is deleted.

Projects An LLM gateway and a prescribing system. Both deployed, both linked, one figure each.

SwitchBoard↗ live site, opens in a new tab

An LLM gateway that keeps three providers behind one endpoint, and recognises a question it has already answered.

One endpoint in front of Groq, Google and Anthropic. A semantic cache answers repeat questions instead of the provider, and keys rotate the moment one hits a rate limit. The application layer never learns that a provider degraded.

The cache compares prompts by cosine similarity rather than by hash, which is the only reason it catches a question asked in different words. Swapping a provider is a config change, not a refactor.

PharmaGuard↗ live site, opens in a new tab

Prescribing guidance read off a genomic file, in a system where the model does not get to make the call.

A genomic VCF goes in and CPIC-guided prescribing guidance comes out. A deterministic risk engine makes every clinical call. The model writes one thing, the explanation a clinician reads, and it writes that after the decision is already final.

That constraint is the architecture, not a precaution bolted on afterwards. It is also the only property that makes an LLM admissible here. If the model fails, the clinician loses the prose and keeps the recommendation.

Limits What is not validated, and what I have not done.

Most of what is on this site is hackathon- or coursework-scale. The two engagements under Experience are not, and neither is SwitchBoard, which has served real traffic. I will stand behind the numbers in Fig. 1.

The Kapture work is an internship, and I tuned that serving stack rather than building it.

PharmaGuard has not been through clinical validation. Do not read it as if it had. It shows that the boundary in Fig. 2 can be built. It is not evidence that the recommendations behind it are safe to act on.

The RDSO crawler ran weekly on one small server inside one network. It is correct, not fast, and it has never been asked to be. I have also written it up here from memory of a system I no longer have access to.

I have not trained anything at scale and I have not published. The two reproductions under Projects are the closest I have come to research, and they are small. My work starts at the model boundary and goes outward. Routing, retrieval, ingest, failover, evaluation. I would rather write that down than let a résumé imply otherwise.
