PLATE 01 — TIMELINE

SwitchBoard

LLM GATEWAY · MULTI-PROVIDER ROUTING

One endpoint in front of OpenAI, Gemini and Groq. Requests route by cost and health; when a provider degrades, the fallback chain takes over without the application layer knowing a thing.

Retries, quota accounting and centralised logging sit in the gateway, so swapping a provider is a config change, not a refactor.

ROLEDesign + build
SURFACESingle OpenAI-shaped API
FAILOVERHealth-scored chain, automatic
STACKPython · FastAPI · Redis

PLATE 02 — BOUNDARY

PharmaGuard

PHARMACOGENOMICS · CLINICAL DECISION SUPPORT

Genomic VCF in, CPIC-guided prescribing guidance out. Every clinical call is made by a deterministic risk engine. The model never gets a vote.

The LLM is confined to one job on the far side of the line: turning a decided recommendation into prose a clinician will actually read. If it fails, you lose the explanation, not the recommendation.

INPUTVCF → PharmCAT diplotypes
DECIDESRule engine, CPIC tables
EXPLAINSLlama 3.3, constrained
OUTFastAPI → EHR-shaped JSON
FIG. 1.2RESPONSIBILITY BOUNDARY

Fig. 1.2 The single arrow crossing the line is the whole design. Model output is downstream of the decision, never upstream of it.

Dropout, from scratch

NUMPY ONLY · NO FRAMEWORK

A full network built from nothing but Python and NumPy — forward pass, backprop, and dropout reimplemented from the paper rather than imported.

The point wasn't the accuracy. It was reproducing the figure: watching validation loss peel away from training loss without dropout, and stay pinned to it with.

PLATE 05 — RETRIEVAL

Hybrid Semantic Search

FAISS + TF-IDF OVER PDFs

Dense retrieval finds documents that mean the query. Sparse retrieval finds documents that say it. Neither alone is enough on a PDF corpus full of product codes and proper nouns.

So both run, and the ranked union goes to the reader. The interesting documents are the ones only one method found.

DENSEFAISS over embeddings
SPARSETF-IDF, exact tokens
FUSIONRanked union
UIStreamlit, PDF upload

04

Limits

The part most portfolios leave out.

Most of what's above is hackathon-scale or coursework-scale. SwitchBoard has run real traffic; PharmaGuard has not been through a clinical validation and should not be treated as if it had.

I'm strongest from the model boundary outward — routing, retrieval, evaluation, the plumbing that decides what a model is even allowed to touch. I have not trained anything at scale, and I'd rather say so than imply otherwise.

If you're putting a model somewhere it can do damage, I'd like to hear about it.
