Design exploration · round 4

Three directions for the portfolio.

All three hold the same type and palette (Inter Tight + JetBrains Mono, near-black + lime) and the same content. Only the presentation changes. Pick by which one you'd actually want to send someone.

WHAT CHANGED FROM ROUND 3

  • The generic diagram is gone "The shape of most things I build" over a stock app skeleton. It flattened five different systems into one CRUD-plus-LLM claim. The hero is type only now.
  • No two figures are alike Three identical box-and-arrow diagrams read as wallpaper by the third one. Each project now gets the figure that actually explains it.
  • A Limits section exists Says plainly what hasn't been validated and what hasn't been done at scale. Slop never admits a limit, so admitting one makes everything else more credible.
  • Figures are labelled schematic Anything not measured says so. Swap in real numbers when you have them — do not ship invented benchmarks.

P1

Plates

Prose on black, every figure on a warm paper plate. The light/dark alternation running down the page is what kills the monotony — the figures become the spine of the layout instead of decoration hanging off it.

PAPER FIGURES · 5 FIGURE TYPES · STICKY RAIL
Safest. Reads as published research. Least likely to age badly, least likely to be remembered.

P2

Executed notebook

In[n] / Out[n] cell rhythm — a prose cell, a real code cell, then the output it produced. Three scroll regions: cell index on the left, cells in the middle, and a runtime panel on the right that tracks where you are.

3 REGIONS · CODE + OUTPUT CELLS · LIVE KERNEL PANEL
Recommended. Most visual of the three, and native to how you actually work — which is why it reads as yours rather than as a template.

P3

Sideways panes

Reads horizontally. Panes pin at the left as you move right and collapse into vertical spines, so your whole path stays on screen. Vertical scroll inside a pane, horizontal scroll across them.

H-SCROLL DECK · STACKING SPINES · 2-AXIS SCROLL
Riskiest. Most memorable by a distance, but horizontal reading loses some people instantly and needs a separate mobile story.
