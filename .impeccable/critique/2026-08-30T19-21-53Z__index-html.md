## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: authored for this product, with one loud exception and one structural exception.**

Three things here could not be lifted onto another product without breaking. The figures (index.html:875-913, 937-987) are arguments rather than ornament: Fig. 2 draws an *absence*, one arrow crossing a dashed line and none crossing back, and the systems claim lands without a sentence. They are built from the page's own token classes, so one artefact re-themes instead of two exports. Limits (1077-1084) spends the page's only bold emphasis on the sentence that costs the most. The evidence-type label in each plate head (`measurement` / `architecture`) tells the reader what class of claim is coming before they read it. No unrelated product ships that.

The loud exception is the typewriter headline (index.html:711, 1349-1370). `studio/STRATEGY.md:449` bans it by name, in the project's own words: "Typewriter / text-scramble effects on the headline." I verified the line. It is the one element on this page that would be at home on any bootcamp portfolio, and it sits on the sentence carrying his identity.

The structural exception is the one that matters most. Screen one at 1440×900 is: name, animated headline, four unlabelled circles, two sentences, and a five-row table of contents. Swap the copy and that is a designer's portfolio, a consultant's page, or an agency about page. **The most product-specific asset on the page sits behind the most product-generic screen.** That inversion is the central design failure, and it is a composition decision, not a content one.

Coherence is very high, almost to a fault. Every bounded region is a 1px hairline; every small label is uppercase mono. The cost is that hierarchy is carried almost entirely by type size, with no weight, ground, or density contrast to lean on. When everything is a hairline box, nothing is a button — which is why the résumé, the page's declared success metric, is measurably the weakest object in the rail.

## Overall Impression

This is the best-executed portfolio I have reviewed against this rubric, and it is losing to itself.

The craft floor is extraordinary and I want to be specific about that, because the score above will otherwise read as harsher than the page deserves. Assessment B measured 169 text elements per theme and found **zero WCAG AA contrast failures in either ground** (lowest 4.81:1 light, 5.15:1 dark). Zero horizontal overflow at nine widths from 320 to 1920. Zero console errors. Thirty-one focusable elements, all native `<a>` or `<button>`, all with visible focus rings, no positive tabindex, no `div` pretending to be a control. Seventeen of seventeen motion rules gated on `prefers-reduced-motion`. No-JS degradation verified intact. Zero reader-visible em dashes, zero colons as connectors. Four network requests, all fonts.

And the page still fails its own user. The stated visitor decides in well under a minute, arriving from a resume link. What that visitor gets in the first viewport is a menu. The proof — the thing PRODUCT.md Principle 1 calls "the proof" — is one click and one scroll away in panel mode, roughly 3,400px away in scroll mode, and 8,344px of document below that on a phone.

**The single biggest opportunity: move one checkable fact above the table of contents.** Everything else on this list is refinement.

## What's Working

1. **The figures are evidence, and they are structurally native.** Fig. 2 (index.html:937-987) works because it draws an absence: the eye looks for a return arrow, does not find one, and the architecture argument lands. It is rendered from the page's own token classes, so it re-themes with the ground at no cost, and `<title id="f2t">` + `<desc id="f2d">` restate the full argument in prose so a non-sighted reader gets the claim rather than a stub. Assessment B verified all four ids resolve inside their own SVG. Analytical content, token-native rendering, and a real text equivalent together are rare in production, and they are why this page is not interchangeable.

2. **Limits is a credibility mechanism, correctly weighted.** It works because the page spends its only bold emphasis on the sentence that costs him most ("PharmaGuard has not been through clinical validation"), and because the admissions are specific rather than performative: "I tuned that serving stack rather than building it," "written up here from memory of a system I no longer have access to." A generic humility section reads as a move. These read as facts, which is what makes them convert.

3. **The two grounds are two readings, and both measure clean.** The accent was re-hued for dark (`#B23B24` → `#E8674A`) rather than reused, because the light accent measures 1.7:1 on the dark ground. Add a print stylesheet that resets both grounds to paper. This is the same discipline the figures show, applied to the substrate, and it is why the page reads as made rather than themed.

### 2. [P1] Figure annotation text renders at 7.4–8.8px across the entire 768–1250px band
**index.html:430, 491-495, 604-608.** Convergent finding: the detector flagged 31 `tiny-text` hits at 9.5–11.5px in the SVG labels, and the design review computed what those become after viewBox scaling. The SVG has an 840-unit viewBox scaled to plate width, and the main column is `viewport − 380` below 1280px. So 1100px gives an effective **7.65px**, 1200px gives 8.8px, 768px gives 7.4px. Only at ≥1280px does it reach 9.7px.

I verified the cause: the `overflow-x:auto` + `min-width:660px` scroll frame that rescues the figures exists **only inside the `max-width:767px` block** (index.html:604-608). Between 768px and 1280px there is no scroll escape and no minimum size — the drawing just shrinks.

**Why it matters:** that band covers tablets, 13-inch laptops at non-default scaling, and any non-maximised window. The figures are the page's entire differentiator and they arrive unreadable to a large share of the target audience.

**Fix:** raise the scroll frame from `max-width:767px` to `max-width:1279px` with `min-width` around 760px, and show `.scroll-hint` across the same range. Move `.scroll-hint` *inside* the scrolling `.plate` while you are there — it currently sits below the element it describes, so the affordance is detached from the thing it affords.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt index.html`

### 3. [P1] The panel swap replaces the main content with no announcement, and the rail is not a tab pattern
**index.html:154-166, 408, 1179-1190, 1283-1291.** Assessment B measured it: `role="tablist"` 0, `role="tab"` 0, `role="tabpanel"` 0, no `aria-selected`, no `aria-controls` on rail links. Above 1100px those links `preventDefault` and swap `[data-active]`, so they *behave* as tabs while being announced as ordinary navigation links. There is no `aria-live`, no focus move, and no region boundary. A screen-reader user hears "current page, Projects," then arrows into entirely different content with no event telling them anything happened. No `<section>` carries `aria-labelledby`, so in a design whose whole model is one section at a time, the sections are not exposed as regions.

For sighted users the same swap is the ~415ms blank column (`rise`, 280ms + 135ms stagger, `both` fill — verified at index.html:118-130), which reads as a page failing to load. And `display:none` on inactive sections means Cmd+F for "vLLM" from the Projects panel returns nothing.

**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md commits to WCAG AA and full keyboard reachability. The page delivers on every mechanical part of that and then leaves its single most important state change unannounced.

**Fix:** either adopt the ARIA tabs pattern properly (`role="tablist"` on the `ul`, `role="tab"` + `aria-selected` + `aria-controls` on the links, `role="tabpanel"` + `aria-labelledby` on the sections, roving tabindex and arrow-key handling), or move focus to the newly activated section's heading and give each `<section>` an `aria-labelledby`. Add `aria-labelledby` to all six sections regardless. Consider shortening the `rise` stagger so the blank window drops well under 200ms.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden index.html`

### 5. [P2] The résumé is the lowest-contrast object in the rail, and two taps away on touch
**index.html:204-215, 538-543, 551-573, 694.** `--rule` measures **1.36:1** against the light ground (B measured 20 of 24 borders below the 3:1 non-text threshold; dark is 1.66:1). PRODUCT.md Principle 3 says the résumé is never more than one action away and names the download as a success metric. The fix already exists in the file and is not applied to the more important instance: `.contact-links a.first` (index.html:487) uses `border-color:var(--ink-3)` at 4.83:1.

**Why it matters:** the page's primary conversion element is drawn in the weakest visual language it owns and gated behind the least discoverable control it owns.

**Fix:** set `.rail-foot .cv` border to `var(--ink-3)`; hoist `.cv` out of `#rail-body` so it sits on the strip beside the nav button at ≥390px; pad `a.cta` and both `a.figref` to at least 24px of hit height.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish index.html`

### Adjudicated down: the panel/media-query desync
Assessment A reported reproducing `data-panels` absent at 1440px and present at 375px, and filed it as a P1 with a stale `aria-current` on Contact. **Assessment B investigated the same behaviour and proved it is a harness artifact.** B registered its own independent `MediaQueryList` listener and showed it missed the identical false→true transition, and observed the emulated viewport drifting between calls (a nominal 900px read back as 1099, then 1440). Chrome does not reliably dispatch MQL `change` events under CDP `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride`. B's fresh-load verification at 375 / 1099 / 1100 / 1440 was deterministic and passed cleanly in both regimes.

B's evidence is stronger, so I am **not** filing this as a defect. The residual is a real but minor code smell, filed as **[P3]**: `apply()` has exactly one trigger (index.html:1303) and there is no idempotent reconciliation, so any dropped `change` event persists until reload. The two-line insurance A proposed is cheap and worth taking — add `if (wide.matches !== (root.dataset.panels === 'on')) apply();` to `schedule()`'s rAF callback — but it fixes a hypothetical, not an observed failure.

## Questions to Consider

1. **If the panel model is right, why is About the guaranteed screen?** Panel mode means the reader sees exactly one screen for certain. You chose the only one containing no evidence. What if it were SwitchBoard — name, the 0.9144 result, Fig. 1 — with the biography demoted to a panel a reader opts into?
2. **What if the desktop layout were two columns, not one panel?** The rail is 236px and `main` is 900px of a 1280px shell, but prose caps at 66ch. A persistent figure column beside the prose column would make the proof visible *while* the argument is read — which is what STRATEGY.md §3 originally specified.
3. **The two throughput numbers are off the page because they cannot be honestly averaged. What if the honest move is a figure that shows exactly that?** Two labelled bars on one axis, `benchmark harness` and `production traffic`, split stated in the caption. That is the same evidence-type discipline the plate heads already invented. Refusing to draw them is one reading of honesty; drawing the distinction is a stronger one, and it puts a magnitude on the page.
4. **What has to be true for the résumé to be a filled button?** The page already breaks its own rectangle rule for four social links and defends it in a comment. Why not for the one object the product defines success by?
5. **Should Limits come earlier?** It is the page's trust instrument and it sits fifth of six. A reader who leaves at second 40 never reaches it.
6. **Does the recruiter ever need to know a section is called "Ledger"?** The `.peek` rows already carry the real names. What if the rail carried those and the abstractions disappeared?
